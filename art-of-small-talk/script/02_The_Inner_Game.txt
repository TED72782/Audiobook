Lesson two. The inner game.

Before technique, we need to deal with fear. Most people do not lack things to say. They lack permission to say them. Something inside holds the words back. Let us look at what that something is, because most of it turns out to be mistaken.

Mistake number one: they do not want to be bothered.

Psychologists in Chicago once ran a now famous experiment with commuters. Some were asked to start a conversation with a stranger on the train. Others were asked to sit in silence as usual. Beforehand, people predicted that talking to a stranger would be the less pleasant ride. Afterward, the people who talked reported the more pleasant ride. And the strangers they talked to were, on the whole, glad of it.

We consistently underestimate how willing other people are to talk. Each of us sits there assuming that everyone else prefers silence, while most of those other people are assuming the same thing about us. A room full of people who would enjoy a chat, each politely leaving the others alone.

Mistake number two: I made a bad impression.

Another line of research found what its authors called the liking gap. After two strangers talk, each one tends to believe the other liked them less than they really did. We replay our stumbles. The other person never noticed them. They were too busy worrying about their own. So here is a rule you may rely on: you came across better than you think.

Mistake number three: I have to be interesting.

No. You have to be interested. This is the most freeing idea in the course. If you believe you must entertain, every conversation is a performance, and performances are frightening. If you believe your job is to be curious about the other person, the pressure moves off you entirely. You do not need material. They are the material.

Mistake number four: an awkward moment is a disaster.

It is not. A pause, a joke that falls flat, a name forgotten. These things happen in nearly every conversation between people who do not know each other. They last two seconds. The only thing that makes them worse is treating them as serious. A small smile and the next sentence, and they are gone.

Now, with those four mistakes cleared away, here are three attitudes to put in their place.

First, be the host. Wherever you are, imagine it is your party. A host does not wonder whether to speak to a guest standing alone. A host walks over, because looking after people is the job. You can adopt that role anywhere, with no one's permission. It turns your attention outward, toward someone else's comfort, and self consciousness cannot survive when your attention is outward.

Second, be generous. Go first. Smile first, say hello first, offer your name first, share something first. Someone has to take the small risk, and the person who takes it is giving a gift. Most people are relieved when you do.

Third, be light about the result. Not every conversation will catch. Some people are tired, or shy, or having a terrible day that has nothing to do with you. If an exchange fizzles, it cost you thirty seconds, and you were kind to someone. That is still a good use of thirty seconds. Treat each attempt as practice, and none of them can fail.

A word for the introverts. Being introverted does not mean being bad at this. Many of the best conversationalists are quiet people. They listen closely, and they ask real questions. What introverts need is not a new personality. They need a plan, a way to save energy, and permission to leave when they are done. All three are coming.

And a word about your body, since nerves live there. Before you walk into a room, let your breath out slowly, longer than you breathed in. Drop your shoulders. Lift your eyes off the floor. Let your face rest in something close to a smile. You are not faking confidence. You are sending your own nervous system the signal that nothing here is dangerous. Which is true.

Your exercise for lesson two. Three times tomorrow, greet someone you would normally pass in silence. A real hello, with eye contact and a smile. Nothing more is required. You are only proving to yourself that the first step is safe.
